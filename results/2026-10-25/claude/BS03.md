I answered this from general knowledge and did not search live listings, so check current prices and stock yourself. Oval blue sapphire pendants with a diamond halo are a common style in the US. Where to look:

**Online specialists (best selection and sapphire detail)**
- **Blue Nile** (bluenile.com): Large catalog of sapphire pendants and halo styles. Clear specs, and returns are generally allowed.
- **James Allen** (jamesallen.com): Lets you view each stone in 360° video. Offers oval sapphire pendants and custom settings.
- **Brilliant Earth** (brilliantearth.com): Sells sapphire halo pendants and lets you choose the metal. Known for traceable and recycled-metal sourcing.
- **Ritani, Whiteflash, Clean Origin**: Good for custom or lab-grown options.
- **Etsy**: Many independent jewelers sell oval sapphire halo pendants, often at lower prices. Check seller reviews and ask for a gemstone report.

**Mainstream and department retailers (easy returns, mid-range prices)**
- **Kay, Zales, Jared, Helzberg Diamonds, Macy's, Nordstrom, Costco**: Costco often has strong value on sapphire-and-diamond pendants.
- **Amazon and Walmart (jewelry)**: Cheaper, but verify that the seller is reputable and that the sapphire is natural, not lab-created or treated.

**High-end**
- **Tiffany & Co., Cartier, Graff, Bulgari**: Premium quality and pricing.
- **Local independent jewelers or a GIA Graduate Gemologist**: A good route for custom work and for seeing the stone in person.

**What to check before buying**
- **Natural vs. lab-created sapphire**: Lab-created stones are much cheaper. Prices below assume you are comparing like with like.
- **Heat treatment**: Most natural sapphires are heated. This is normal if disclosed. Untreated stones cost much more.
- **Color**: Look for a vivid, medium-to-medium-dark royal or cornflower blue. Avoid stones that look too dark or too washed out.
- **Certification**: For stones over about 1 carat, ask for a GIA, AGL, or similar lab report.
- **Setting**: 14k or 18k white gold or platinum shows off blue sapphire best. Check that the halo diamonds are well set.
- **Typical prices**: Under about $500 buys a small or lab-created stone. $500 to $2,000 buys a good natural 0.5 to 1 ct stone. Fine, untreated, or larger stones cost much more.
