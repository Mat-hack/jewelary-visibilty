I didn't search live listings, so check current prices and stock yourself. These are well-known US sellers of London blue topaz rings.

**Online jewelers (widest selection)**
- **Blue Nile** (bluenile.com): Offers gemstone rings, including blue topaz, in sterling silver and gold. Good for higher quality and has clear return policies.
- **James Allen** (jamesallen.com): Mostly known for diamonds. It also carries some gemstone rings and custom settings.
- **Brilliant Earth** (brilliantearth.com): Sells blue topaz in modern and ethically sourced settings. It tends to cost more.
- **Etsy** (etsy.com): Many independent makers sell custom London blue topaz rings, often at lower prices. Check seller reviews and ask about stone treatment and metal purity before you buy.
- **Amazon**: Lots of budget options in sterling silver. Quality varies, so read the reviews closely and choose sellers that give a clear stone description.

**Budget-friendly and mainstream**
- **Kay, Zales, Jared, Macy's, and Helzberg Diamonds**: Carry blue topaz rings in stores and online, usually in sterling silver or 10k/14k gold.
- **JCPenney and Kohl's**: Lower-priced topaz rings.

**Gem and specialty retailers**
- **Gem Rock Auctions** and **Gemstone sellers on Etsy or eBay**: Good if you want to pick a loose stone and have it set.
- **Local independent jewelers**: Many will source a London blue topaz and make a custom ring, so you can look at the stone in person.

**Tips**
- Nearly all London blue topaz is treated (irradiated and heated) to get its deep color. This is standard and permanent, but the seller should say so.
- Ask for the carat weight, cut, metal (sterling silver, 10k, 14k, or 18k), and the return policy.
- Prices run from about $30 to $100 for small silver rings and from several hundred to a few thousand dollars for larger stones in gold.
- Deep, inky "London" blue is darker than "Swiss" and "Sky" blue topaz. Check the product photos so you get the shade you want.
