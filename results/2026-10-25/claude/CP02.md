**Natural vs lab-grown sapphire**

**What's the same:** Both are real sapphire, meaning corundum (aluminum oxide) with trace elements that give the color. They have the same chemistry, crystal structure, hardness (9 on the Mohs scale) and optical properties. A lab-grown sapphire isn't a "fake" or a simulant like glass or cubic zirconia.

**What's different:**
- **Origin:** Natural sapphires form over millions of years underground and are mined. Lab-grown ones are made in weeks or months by flame fusion (Verneuil), flux, hydrothermal or Czochralski methods.
- **Inclusions:** Natural stones usually have internal features such as silk (fine rutile needles), mineral crystals and growth zoning. Lab stones are often cleaner. Under magnification they may show curved growth lines or gas bubbles, depending on the method. Only a gemologist or lab can reliably tell them apart.
- **Price:** Lab-grown sapphires typically cost a small fraction of a comparable natural stone, often 80-95% less, and the gap is widest for fine, large stones. Natural prices depend on color, clarity, size, origin and treatment.
- **Rarity and resale:** Natural sapphires are finite, and fine untreated stones can hold or gain value. Lab-grown sapphires are unlimited in supply and have little to no resale value.
- **Treatment:** Most natural sapphires are heat-treated, which is accepted and normal. Untreated stones are rarer and worth a premium. Lab-grown stones usually don't need treatment.
- **Environmental and ethical impact:** Lab-grown stones avoid mining impacts, though growing them uses energy. Natural stones vary in sourcing. Ask for traceability if it matters to you.

**Which should you buy?**
- **Choose lab-grown if** you want the most size and color for the money, you want a clean stone, you care about avoiding mining, or it's for fashion or everyday wear and you don't care about resale.
- **Choose natural if** you value rarity, origin and the stone's geological story, you want something that may hold value, or it's for an heirloom or a meaningful piece such as an engagement ring.
- **Rule of thumb:** Buy for the look and the price you're happy with, and don't expect lab-grown to be an investment.

**Buying tips:**
1. Insist on clear disclosure of natural vs lab-grown and any treatments, in writing.
2. For natural stones above about $1,000, ask for a report from a respected lab (GIA, AGL, SSEF, Gübelin, GRS).
3. Compare stones by color, since that drives value. Look for vivid, evenly saturated blue that isn't too dark or too light.
4. Be wary of "natural" claims at very low prices. They are usually lab-grown, glass-filled or heavily treated.
5. Buy from sellers with a return policy.
