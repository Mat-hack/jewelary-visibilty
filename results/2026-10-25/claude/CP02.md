**Natural vs lab-grown sapphire (US buyer's guide)**

**What's the same.** Both are real sapphire: crystalline aluminum oxide (corundum) with trace elements, such as iron and titanium for blue, that give the color. They have the same hardness (9 on the Mohs scale), the same chemistry and the same optical properties. A lab-grown sapphire isn't glass or a simulant like cubic zirconia.

**What's different**
- **Origin.** Natural stones formed over millions of years and were mined. Lab-grown stones are made in weeks, usually by flame fusion (Verneuil), flux growth or hydrothermal growth.
- **Inclusions.** Natural stones often have tiny needles ("silk"), crystals and growth zoning. Flame-fusion lab stones show curved color bands and gas bubbles. Lab stones can also be very clean.
- **Price.** Lab-grown sapphire is usually a small fraction of the cost of a natural stone of similar size and color. Gem-quality lab sapphire often sells for tens of dollars per carat. Fine natural stones can run from hundreds to thousands of dollars per carat, and more for top Kashmir, Burmese or unheated Ceylon stones.
- **Rarity and resale.** Natural sapphires, especially untreated ones of fine color, are finite and can hold or gain value. Lab-grown stones have little to no resale value and can be made in unlimited quantity.
- **Treatment.** Most natural sapphires are heat-treated. This is accepted and stable, but it should be disclosed. Untreated stones cost much more. Beryllium diffusion and fracture filling are lesser treatments that should lower the price a lot.
- **Identification.** You often can't tell the difference by eye. A gemological lab can separate them using magnification and spectroscopy.

**US disclosure rules.** The FTC Jewelry Guides say a lab-created stone must be clearly disclosed as "laboratory-grown," "laboratory-created" or "[manufacturer]-created." The word "synthetic" is also allowed. Terms like "real," "genuine" or "natural" must not be used for lab-grown stones. Treatments that affect value should also be disclosed.

**Which should you buy?**
- **Choose lab-grown if:**
  - You want the best look for the money, such as a large, vivid blue stone for an engagement ring or everyday wear.
  - You don't care about rarity or resale.
  - You prefer a stone with no mining footprint (though "ethical" claims are not automatic, so ask the seller).
- **Choose natural if:**
  - Origin, rarity and heirloom value matter to you.
  - You may want to resell or pass it down.
  - You can afford it. For the best value, look for heated-only stones from a lab-certified seller.

Either way, sapphire's hardness makes it a good choice for daily wear.

**Buying tips**
1. Ask for a report from a respected lab such as GIA, AGL or SSEF (Gübelin, GRS and Lotus are also reputable). It should state natural or lab-grown, any treatment and, for natural stones, origin where applicable.
2. Get "natural, heated" or "lab-grown" in writing on the invoice.
3. Compare color, saturation, clarity and cut. For blue sapphire, a vivid, medium-to-medium-dark blue with good brightness is the most valuable, and cut quality has a large effect on beauty.
4. Be skeptical of a "natural" sapphire at a very low price. Treated or lab-grown stones are often sold deceptively online.
5. Check the return policy (30 days or more is common). Reputable US sellers include Blue Nile, James Allen, Brilliant Earth and local independent jewelers, but compare prices and read the policy.

**Bottom line.** Buy lab-grown if you want the most beautiful stone for your budget. Buy natural, with a certificate, if you value rarity, origin and long-term value and can pay more. Don't pay natural prices for a stone that isn't documented as natural.
