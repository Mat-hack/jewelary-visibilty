I answered this from general knowledge and didn't check live listings, so confirm current stock, prices and return policies yourself. These are well-known US sellers of blue sapphire halo studs, grouped by budget.

**Fine jewelry (natural sapphire, real gold)**
- **Blue Nile** (bluenile.com): Good selection of sapphire halo studs in 14k and 18k gold or platinum. It offers free shipping and returns.
- **James Allen** (jamesallen.com): Sells sapphire halo studs and has 360° video of each stone.
- **Brilliant Earth** (brilliantearth.com): Offers natural and lab-created sapphire halo studs with an ethical-sourcing focus. You can also customize them.
- **Ross-Simons** (ross-simons.com): Often has sales on sapphire and diamond-halo studs at moderate prices.
- **Zales, Kay, Jared**: Stores plus online. Sapphire-and-diamond halo studs are easy to find, and the stores offer in-person returns. Expect frequent promotions.
- **Costco jewelry** (costco.com): Competitive prices on gold and sapphire studs.

**Mid-range and budget**
- **Amazon**: Large selection, from sterling silver with lab or simulated sapphire up to real gold. Check that the seller is reputable and that the listing states "natural" or "lab-created" and the metal karat.
- **Etsy**: Good for handmade or custom halo studs. Check seller reviews and ask for the stone's origin and treatment.
- **Macy's, Nordstrom, Kohl's**: Lower-priced sapphire and diamond-accent halo studs in sterling silver or 10k–14k gold.

**What to check before buying**
- **Natural vs. lab-created**: Both are real sapphire. Lab-created costs much less.
- **Treatment**: Most natural sapphires are heat-treated, which is normal. It should be disclosed.
- **Metal**: Look for 14k or 18k gold, platinum or sterling silver.
- **Size and color**: Sapphire carat weight (for example 0.5 to 1 ct total) and color description, such as royal blue or cornflower.
- **Backs**: Screw backs are more secure than push backs.
- **Returns**: A 30-day return window or longer, plus a stated warranty.
