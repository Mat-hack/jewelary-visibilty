Here are places in the USA where you can buy a gemstone ring and pick the quality grade. This comes from my general knowledge. I didn't search the web, so check current offerings and prices yourself.

**Online retailers with customizable rings and gemstone grade selection**
- **Blue Nile** (bluenile.com): You build a ring by choosing the setting, then the stone. It's strongest for diamonds. It also carries sapphires, rubies and emeralds, with filters for origin, treatment, clarity and cut.
- **James Allen** (jamesallen.com): It offers 360° HD video of each individual stone, so you can judge clarity and color yourself. It has diamonds and some colored gemstones, and a ring builder.
- **Brilliant Earth** (brilliantearth.com): It sells sapphires, lab-grown and natural stones, with a focus on ethical sourcing. You can filter by color, clarity, cut and treatment.
- **With Clarity** (withclarity.com): It has a large selection of diamonds and gemstones, with filters by grade.
- **Ritani, Whiteflash and Adiamor**: These are good for custom ring settings with graded stones.
- **Gemstone specialists** such as **Gem Rock Auctions**, **Ruby Lane** and **Etsy** sellers (check reviews): You can choose the exact stone, then have it set. Quality varies by seller, so look for lab reports.
- **Gemstones and loose-stone dealers** such as **Gemporia**, **JTV (Jewelry Television)** and **Blue Nile's loose gemstone section**: You choose the stone and then the setting. Check return policies.

**Local or in-person options**
- **Independent jewelers and custom jewelry studios** (search "custom jeweler near me"). A local jeweler can show you stones side by side and set one in a ring. Ask whether they are a **GIA Graduate Gemologist** or **AGS-certified**.
- **The Jewelers Board of Trade** and the **American Gem Society (AGS)** (americangemsociety.org) list vetted members.
- **Chain jewelers** such as Signet brands (Kay, Jared, Zales) have less flexibility on gemstone grades.
- **Gem and jewelry shows** such as the Tucson Gem Show (early in the year) and other regional shows. You can often buy direct from dealers.

**Tips for choosing the quality grade**
- **Ask for a lab report** from GIA, AGL (American Gemological Laboratories) or Gübelin. These report origin and treatment (heated, untreated and so on). They matter most for sapphires, rubies and emeralds.
- **Know the grading factors for colored stones**: color is the most important, then clarity, cut and carat weight. Colored stones are not graded on the same D-to-Z scale as diamonds.
- **Check for treatments**. Heat treatment is common and accepted for sapphires and rubies. Fracture filling, diffusion or glass filling lowers value a lot.
- **Compare natural and lab-grown**. Lab-grown stones cost much less at the same visual quality. Make sure the listing says which one it is.
- **Check the return policy**. A 30-day return window, free resizing and insurance are worth having.

**Suggested path**
1. Decide on the stone type and budget.
2. Browse a ring builder such as Blue Nile, James Allen or Brilliant Earth to see grade options and prices.
3. If you want a one-of-a-kind or higher-end stone, ask a local GIA-credentialed jeweler to source it or to look over an online stone before you buy.
